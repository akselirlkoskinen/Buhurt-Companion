# Footwork Fundamentals

Good footwork is the foundation of everything in buhurt — it controls distance, creates angles, and keeps you balanced under pressure. This guide covers the core concepts every fighter should drill before focusing on weapons or grappling.

---

## 1. The Fighting Stance

Your default position when facing an opponent.

**Feet placement:**
- Shoulder-width apart, slightly staggered (dominant foot back)
- Weight balanced 50/50 — avoid leaning forward or back
- Knees slightly bent — never locked

**Body alignment:**
- Hips facing roughly 45° to your opponent (reduces target profile)
- Shield arm forward, weapon arm loaded back
- Head upright — maintain full field of view

**Common errors:**
- Feet too wide → slow lateral movement
- Feet too narrow → easy to push off balance
- Upright knees → absorbs impact poorly, harder to sprawl

---

## 2. Step-and-Replace

The basic method for moving without crossing your feet.

**Forward:**
1. Step the front foot forward
2. Replace the back foot to restore your staggered stance

**Backward:**
1. Step the back foot back
2. Replace the front foot

**Lateral (right):**
1. Step the right foot right
2. Replace the left foot

**Drill:** Set two cones 3 m apart. Step-and-replace forward to the first cone, lateral to the second, backward to start. 5 × without stopping.

> Never let your feet come together during movement — you lose stability at the moment you are most likely to be hit.

---

## 3. Circling

Moving around your opponent to create angles and avoid their dominant weapon.

- Circle away from their weapon hand when possible
- Keep constant pressure on the ball of your foot
- Combine small steps — do not lunge sideways

**Drill:** Partner drill. One person stands still. You circle slowly around them, maintaining the same distance (roughly 1.5 m), for 60 seconds. Then swap.

---

## 4. The Short Shuffle

Used to close distance quickly without telegraphing.

1. Front foot takes a small step forward
2. Back foot follows immediately — do not pause in between
3. Both feet return to stance simultaneously

The key is that both feet move almost at the same time, so there is no single moment when you are flat-footed or unbalanced.

**Application:** Use to close the last 30 cm before weapon contact or grappling range. The short shuffle is harder to read than a full step.

---

## 5. Angles — The T-Step

Instead of moving straight back under pressure, create an angle.

1. An opponent pushes or attacks straight at you
2. Step your back foot to the side (roughly 45°)
3. Your front foot pivots in the same direction
4. You are now off the centerline and they are facing empty space

**Why it works:** Forces your opponent to redirect their attack, which takes a fraction of a second longer than continuing the same line. That gap is your opportunity.

---

## 6. In Armour

Full plate changes movement significantly:

- Centre of gravity is higher
- Extra weight makes changes of direction slower
- Visibility is limited — use head turns to compensate
- Footwork efficiency matters more, not less

**Armour drill:** Put on your full kit and walk the step-and-replace drill at half speed. Focus on feeling balanced at every point. Build up to normal speed over several sessions.

## Training Frequency

| Stage | Recommendation |
|-------|---------------|
| New fighter | 10 min footwork at start of every session |
| Intermediate | Dedicated 20-min drill session once a week |
| Pre-tournament | Shadow sparring with footwork focus × 3 per week |
