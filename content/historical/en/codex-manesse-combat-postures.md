# Combat Postures in the Codex Manesse

## About the Source

The **Codex Manesse** (formally *Große Heidelberger Liederhandschrift*) is a Middle High German manuscript compiled approximately 1300–1340 in Zürich. It is the largest and most comprehensive collection of medieval German love poetry (*Minnelieder*) and is renowned for its 137 full-page miniatures depicting named poets.

Many of these miniatures include combat scenes — tournaments, duels, and wrestling — that provide visual evidence of fighting techniques and equipment from early 14th-century German-speaking lands.

**Digitised access:** [digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/cpg848)

## Selected Miniatures — Combat Relevance

### Folio 17r — Heinrich von Veldeke
A mounted joust scene. Notable for the depiction of early-13th-century great helms and lance technique. The grip and couched lance position are clearly rendered.

**Relevance to modern buhurt:** Helm shape (barrel-style) and visor openings as worn in early tournament formats.

---

### Folio 52r — Walther von der Vogelweide
Two knights engaged with swords on foot. The illustration shows:
- A high guard on the left figure (sword raised above the right shoulder)
- A middle guard (*Mittelhut*) on the right figure
- Both figures in mail hauberks with surcoats

**Relevance:** Demonstrates guard positions consistent with later Liechtenauer tradition. Useful reference for authenticity submissions — surcoat over mail, no plate visible.

---

### Folio 82v — Der Hardegger
Wrestling scene without weapons. Two figures grapple from an upright clinch. Observe:
- Collar grip (right hand grips opponent's collar/aventail area)
- Hip position — both fighters' hips slightly forward, weight distributed for leverage
- Footwork — staggered stance even in the clinch

**Relevance:** Primary visual source for period wrestling (*Ringen*) in buhurt. Hip position and grip location are directly applicable to modern rules formats.

---

### Folio 124r — Reinmar der Alte
A jousting scene with broken lances, showing the moment of impact. The body posture of both riders under impact is rare photographic-equivalent evidence for how mounted combatants absorbed strikes.

## Equipment Notes

Across the combat miniatures, recurring features include:

| Item | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Helms | Great helms predominate; some pot helms in wrestling scenes |
| Mail | Full hauberk + coif standard; chausses visible |
| Surcoats | Long, split at the hem for mounted use; heraldic designs |
| Swords | Straight, double-edged; some with prominent pommels suggesting hand-and-a-half grips |
| Shields | Heater shields in tournament; some kite shields in older-style scenes |

---

## Limitations

- The Manesse miniatures are art, not technical manuals. Proportions and positions may be idealised.
- Illustrators were not necessarily fighters — cross-reference with fight manuals (*Fechtbücher*) for technique verification.
- The manuscript spans several decades; equipment depicted is not uniform in period.

## Further Sources for Cross-Reference
